USER GUIDE

Oxygène Studio is a small 1970s-style synth studio. You play sounds on a keyboard, build short musical parts, and lay those parts out on a timeline to make a whole piece.

The idea in one minute

  • There are six tracks: Drums, Bass, Strings, Lead, FX (one-shot space effects) and Wind.
  • A part is a short pattern for one track, 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars long: a drum beat, a bass line, a few chords.
  • The timeline (SONG tab) shows the whole piece, bar by bar. You put parts on it, move them by dragging, and stretch them to make them repeat.
  • The same part can sit on the timeline many times. Change its notes once and every place changes with it.
  • The six tabs: PLAY to play by hand, SOUND to choose and shape sounds, SONG for the timeline, EDIT to write the notes of a part, MIX for levels and echo, FILE to save, open and export.

Want to hear something first? Go to FILE, pick a demo under “Start from”, and press ▶. Then look at the SONG tab to see how it is put together.

Your first piece, step by step

  1. Start clean. FILE → New empty song. (Undo brings the old one back if you did not mean it.)
  2. Add a drum beat. SONG → tap the empty Drums track at bar 1 → at the bottom choose 1 bar. You land in the editor. Open Rhythm box… and pick a rhythm. Press ▶ to hear it loop, ■ to stop.
  3. Make it last. Go back to SONG. Drag the right edge of the drum part to the right until it covers 8 bars. The beat now repeats for 8 bars.
  4. Add a bass line. Tap the empty Bass track at bar 1 → 2 bars. In the editor, tap squares to write notes: each row is a pitch, each column a step in time. Press ▶ to hear it while you work. Back on SONG, stretch it to 8 bars too.
  5. Add chords. Tap the Strings track at bar 1 → 4 bars. Set the note length to Note: 1 bar, then tap three squares in the same column to make a chord. Do the same in the next bars.
  6. Play a melody by hand. PLAY → choose Lead → pick a sound. Press ● at the top: you hear one bar of click, then the song plays and whatever you play on the keyboard is written onto the Lead track. Press ● again (or ■) to stop.
  7. Add wind. SONG → tap the Wind track where it should start, then drag the right edge to set how long it blows.
  8. Listen to the whole thing. On SONG, tap bar 1 in the row of bar numbers, then press ▶.
  9. Balance it. MIX → set each track’s Level, and add Echo and Reverb for the space sound.
  10. Keep it. FILE → give it a name → Save song file, or Whole song → WAV for an audio file.

The top bar

  • ? opens this guide.
  • The amber numbers (like 3·2) show where the music is: bar 3, beat 2.
  • − 100 + is the tempo in beats per minute. Hold a button to change it quickly.
  • ● records what you play (see PLAY below).
  • ▶ starts and stops. What it plays depends on the tab you are on: on EDIT it loops the part you are editing, together with the rest of the song at that spot; on every other tab it plays the song from the playhead.
  • The thin bar under the top row is the output level meter.

PLAY: the keyboard

  • Strings / Lead / Bass chooses which instrument the keyboard plays.
  • Strings can play chords. Lead and Bass play one note at a time, like the old solo synths: a new note replaces the one before.
  • Wind switches the wind on and off by hand. The other pads fire the effects: Zap ↓, Zap ↑, Drops, Swell, Gust from space, and Surf, Rain, Thunder, Birds, Bubbles and Rise (a long climb into the next section).
  • The sound list picks a sound for the instrument. Edit sound opens the SOUND tab.
  • − and + above the keys move the keyboard down or up an octave. You can also slide a finger across the keys.
  • Hold keeps notes sounding after you lift your finger; tap Hold again to let go.
  • Arp (the list that says Arp off) is the running-notes machine. Choose up, down, up + down or random, then hold two or more keys: they play one after another in time with the tempo. The two lists beside it set the speed and how many octaves the pattern climbs. Switch Hold on and you can tap the notes in and take your hands away. With the song playing the pattern stays on the beat, and ● records it into a part. It works on the laser harp and on the test keys of the SOUND tab too.
  • The pitch slide strip above the keys bends the notes: put a finger on it and drag right for higher, left for lower (a little near the middle, up to an octave at the ends). Let go and the pitch comes back. Slides are played by hand; they are not recorded.
  • Laser harp swaps the keys for a fan of green beams and picks the Laser Harp lead sound. Touch a beam to play it, or slide across the beams. There is one beam for each note of the song’s key and scale, so every beam fits; the amber names are the home note. − and + move the beams an octave, recording works the same as with the keys, and you can pick any other sound to play on the beams. Tap Laser harp again for the keys.
  • The dots on the keys mark the notes of the song’s key and scale (set on FILE). Stay on the dots and it will sound right. The amber dot is the home note.

Recording

  1. Choose the instrument. On SONG, tap the bar number where you want to start (or leave it at the start).
  2. Press ●. You hear one bar of click to count you in, then the song plays, with the click continuing.
  3. Play. Your notes are written onto that instrument’s track. If there is already a part under the playhead they go into it; if not, a new part is made for you.
  4. Press ● again to stop recording, or ■ to stop everything.

Notes are snapped to the nearest step, so slightly loose timing is tidied up. Not happy with a take? Press Undo on the SONG tab and try again. The effect pads can be recorded too.

With a computer keyboard: A W S E D F T G Y H U J K play notes, Z and X change octave, Space starts and stops.

SOUND: choose and build sounds

  • Choose Strings, Lead or Bass, then pick a sound from the list. This is the keyboard’s sound: what you play by hand, and what a new part starts with.
  • Every part keeps its own sound. A part takes the keyboard’s sound when it is made or recorded and holds on to it, so changing the keyboard later does not change what is already in the song. To change a part, use its Sound row (on EDIT, or under the timeline on SONG when the part is selected): pick another sound there, or press Edit sound. SOUND then says Part: … at the top, every control changes that part only, and ▶ plays the part so you hear it. Keyboard sound (or tapping the SOUND tab) goes back to the keyboard.
  • The Lead list includes standards such as Piano, Electric Piano, Organ, Electric Guitar, Flute and Brass. They are synth versions, not recordings of the real instruments.
  • Use the note pads at the bottom to hear the sound while you change it.
  • Echo and reverb (the last card before saving) set how much of the sound goes to the tape echo and the reverb. Every sound brings its own amounts: the space leads have plenty of echo, Piano, Organ and Guitar are nearly dry. Turn Echo to 0 for no echo at all.
  • To keep a sound you made: type a name under “Keep it as your own sound” and press Save. Your sounds appear first in the list, in every song. Delete removes one; Revert throws away your changes and goes back to the saved sound.

What the controls do (Lead and Bass)

  • Wave: the raw tone. Saw is bright and buzzy, Square hollow, Triangle and Sine soft, Pulse nasal, Organ like drawbars, Sync and Sync high the hard, tearing tone of the laser harp, Bell glassy.
  • Osc 2 level / detune / pitch: a second copy of the tone. A little detune makes it fat; pitch 12 adds an octave above, 7 a fifth.
  • Sub level: adds weight one octave below.
  • Cutoff: how bright or dull. Resonance: adds a whistling, “wah” edge at the cutoff.
  • Filter env: how much each note opens bright and then closes. High with a short Decay gives a plucked “bow” sound.
  • Attack: how slowly the note fades in. Decay: how fast it drops after the start. Sustain: the level it holds while the key is down (0 = the note dies away like a piano or guitar). Release: how long it rings after you let go.
  • Glide: slides from one note to the next. Vibrato and Vibrato rate: a wobble in pitch.
  • Drive: overdrive, from a little grit to a distorted guitar.

What the controls do (Strings)

  • Brightness, Wave and Detune set the basic tone. 4′ octave adds an octave above, 16′ octave one below.
  • Choir: Voice turns the strings into singing voices, Vowel chooses Aah, Ooh or Eee. The choir sounds in the list use it.
  • Attack and Release: how slowly the chord swells in and fades out.
  • Ensemble: the shimmering “many players” effect.
  • Phaser rate / depth / mix: the slow sweeping swirl that makes the classic space-strings sound. Turn the mix to 0 for plain strings or organ.

SONG: the timeline

Time runs left to right in bars. Each track has its own row. The coloured blocks are parts.

  • Add a part: tap an empty spot on a track. At the bottom choose a length for a new part (you go straight to the editor), or under “or place:” pick a part you already made.
  • Move a part: drag it left or right. It will not move onto another part.
  • Make it longer or shorter: drag its right edge. Longer than the part itself and it repeats; shorter and it is cut off.
  • Tap a part to select it. The bottom bar then offers:
    • Edit notes: open it in the editor.
    • Repeat after: put the same part again right after it.
    • Make own copy: turn this one into a separate part, so you can change it here (a variation) without changing the others.
    • Remove: take it off the timeline. The part itself is kept and can be placed again.
    • Sound (Strings, Lead and Bass parts): the sound this part plays. Pick another from the list, or press Edit sound to change its controls, echo and reverb. A part placed several times has one sound for all of them; press Make own copy first to change just one.
  • Wind has no notes: tap its track to add a stretch of wind, drag the edge for the length.
  • Playhead: tap the row of bar numbers to choose where ▶ starts.
  • Mute: tap a track’s name on the left to silence it; tap again to bring it back.
  • Loop: select a part and press Loop to play just those bars round and round; with nothing selected it loops the whole song. Press Loop again to switch it off.
  • − and + zoom the timeline out and in. Undo takes back the last change.

On a phone, scroll the timeline by dragging on an empty area or on the bar numbers, not on a part (dragging a part moves it).

EDIT: the notes of a part

  • The top row chooses the track. The first list chooses which part; the second sets its length in bars. New part makes an empty one.
  • The grid: time runs left to right, 16 steps to a bar, with a stronger line every beat.
  • Bass, Strings, Lead: each row is a note. First choose the note length (1 step up to 2 bars), then tap a square to put a note there. Tap the start of a note to remove it. The rows are the notes of the song’s scale. Strings can stack notes into chords; Bass and Lead keep one note at a time.
  • Drums: each row is a drum. Tap once for a normal hit, again for a loud (accented) hit, a third time to clear. Rhythm box… fills the part with a ready-made rhythm you can then change. The ones named after a record are in its manner, not exact copies.
  • FX: each row is a space effect; tap to fire it at that step.
  • Sound (Bass, Strings, Lead): the sound this part plays. Pick another from the list right here, or press Edit sound to change its controls, echo and reverb for this part only.
  • ▶ loops the part while you edit, so you hear every change.
  • Clear empties the part. Delete removes the part completely, from the timeline too. Undo brings either back.
  • If a part is “Not on the timeline yet”, press Add to the song to place it after the last part on that track.

MIX: levels and space

  • Each track has Level (loudness), Pan (left or right) and Mute. Drums and Effects also have Echo and Reverb here (how much of them goes to the effects).
  • For Strings, Lead and Bass, echo and reverb belong to each sound, and every part keeps its own: change them with the part’s Sound row → Edit sound → Echo and reverb.
  • Tape echo and reverb sets the effects themselves: echo time (counted in steps, so it stays in time with the tempo), how many repeats, how dull the repeats get, and the size and level of the reverb.
  • Master is the overall volume. Wind level sets how loud the wind is.

For the classic sound: plenty of Echo on the lead sounds, plenty of Reverb on the strings, and keep the bass and drums mostly dry.

FILE: name, key, save

  • Name the song. Key and scale decide which notes get dots on the keyboard and rows in the editor. Notes you already wrote are not changed.
  • The song on screen is kept automatically in this browser, on this device.
  • My songs is your list of saved songs, newest first. Save in My songs puts the song there; tap a name to open it again. Saving again under the same name replaces the earlier one, so change the name first to keep both. Delete asks once more (“Sure?”) before removing a song from the list.
  • Save song file downloads the song as a file: a backup, or a way to move it to another device. It also puts the song in My songs. Open song file loads one.
  • Whole song → WAV makes an audio file of the whole timeline that any player can play.
  • Start from loads a demo. New empty song clears everything. Undo brings the previous song back.

How do I…

…repeat a section?
Stretch each part by its right edge, or select a part and press Repeat after.
…make a variation of a part?
Place the part again, select the new block, press Make own copy, then Edit notes.
…make an intro or a break?
Simply leave tracks empty for those bars. Drag the drum and bass parts to start later, and let the strings and wind play alone first.
…work on just one section?
Select a part in that section and press Loop.
…play chords?
Use the Strings track; it has organ sounds too. Lead and Bass play single notes.
…fix one wrong note in a recording?
On SONG, tap the recorded part → Edit notes, tap the wrong note to remove it and tap the right square.
…change the sound of a track that is already written?
Tap the part on SONG (or open it on EDIT) and pick another sound in its Sound row. The notes stay; only that part’s sound changes. Picking a sound on PLAY changes the keyboard, not the parts already in the song.
…have the same notes with two different sounds?
Place the part again, select the new block, press Make own copy, then pick another sound in its Sound row.
…go back to a song I saved earlier?
FILE → My songs → tap its name.
…make the whole piece longer?
Just place parts further to the right. The timeline grows as you go, up to 256 bars.
…undo a mistake?
Press Undo (on SONG, EDIT or FILE). It takes back the last change only; pressing it again puts the change back.
…move a song or my sounds to another phone?
Songs: Save song file here, Open song file there. The song carries the sounds of its parts with it; on the other device, open a part’s Edit sound and press Save to add that sound to the list there.

Problems

No sound on an iPhone
Switch off silent mode (the ring/silent switch on the side) and turn the volume up. Then tap a key.
Every note has an echo I do not want
For what you play by hand: SOUND tab → scroll down to Echo and reverb → turn Echo down. For a part already in the song: its Sound row → Edit sound → the same two controls.
I changed the sound but the song still plays the old one
PLAY and the SOUND tab set the keyboard’s sound. Parts already in the song keep theirs: change a part with its Sound row on EDIT or SONG.
No sound at all
Sound starts after your first tap. Check MIX: Master is up, the track is not muted, its Level is up.
▶ does nothing
The timeline is empty. Add a part on SONG, or load a demo on FILE.
A part will not move or stretch
Another part on the same track is in the way. Move or shorten that one first.
I changed one part and others changed too
They are the same part placed several times. Use Make own copy on the one you want to be different.
Sound crackles on a phone
Close other apps and tabs. Long Release times and lots of string notes at once are the heaviest.
My songs or sounds are gone
They live in the browser you made them in. Another browser, a private window, or clearing the browser’s data starts empty. Keep song files of anything you care about.
Using it like an app
Open the web page, then choose Add to Home Screen from the browser’s share or menu button. It then also works without a connection.

Words used here

Bar, beat, step
A bar is four beats; a beat is four steps. So a bar has 16 steps.
Part
A short pattern of notes for one track.
Track
One row of the timeline: one instrument.
Playhead
The line showing where the music is, and where ▶ starts.
Tempo
Speed, in beats per minute.
Octave
The same note, higher or lower.
Key and scale
The set of notes that sound right together in this song.

That is everything. The quickest way to learn it is to load a demo and take it apart.
